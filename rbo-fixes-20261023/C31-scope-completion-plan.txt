C31 source review and next verification steps

The original final C31 finding concerns residual reconstruction introducing sharing after the only copy stage. Its fix is the earlier reconstruction pass. The archived row-identity restriction was separately grouped under C31: nullable keys and keyless inputs previously failed before that lifecycle issue. Keep these acceptance claims separate while retaining the archived support limitations in the backlog.

The remaining concrete verification obligation for the new scalar-domain support is exact identity lineage in two existing spilling profiles. Both sides of reconstruction must use the same already-evaluated shared source values. Existing semantic results and a Switch somewhere in the graph do not prove this binding.

One source-confirmed verifier issue was found. The dormant topology probe requires every lambda to have one body, but valid wide projections have multiple bodies. C31-origin-multibody-traversal.patch fixes general traversal while retaining strict stage/projection checks. It does not grant value-lineage proof or resolve lexical block bodies. The patch is dormant and needs independent review.

The dormant C31-scope-observation-generator.py and its installation patch delegate to the existing workload generator. They add only hash-bound topology observations and an allowlisted shape inventory. They never assert any of the three full shared-value proof flags. No workload was rendered or executed during this source review.

Guard precision matters. The repeatable-predicate gate applies to keyless domains, not every keyed residual. The exact-scalar whitelist covers all keyless fields and optional unique keys; it does not newly certify existing nonoptional unique keys. Unsupported keyless Float/Double, opaque/composite types and per-occurrence volatile behavior remain guarded support exclusions. They are not evidence that the original late-sharing failure remains.

Nonoptional Float/Double uniqueness can arise from grouping or deduplication, independently of table primary-key restrictions. Without channel spilling, the stable-copy guard rejects floating grouping keys even for COUNT-only aggregates. With channel spilling, a known nonoptional floating key can be admitted. Primary implementation sources show NaN-aware dictionary comparison, reflexive packed-bit GraceJoin comparison for the same value, and canonicalized block keys. No new NaN self-equality bug was established. This source conclusion does not replace the shared-value lineage proof or certify independently recomputed floating representatives.
